Why I learned these

CFD and FEA aren't just tools I've used in class — they're skills I sought out independently because they directly apply to the work I care about. Working on 3DPAC airframes made aerodynamic simulation immediately relevant. Working on the Baja SAE half-shaft redesign made structural FEA essential. I've been building these skills alongside my coursework to be able to make better design decisions, not just run the software.

Finite Element Analysis (FEA)

FEA lets you predict how a physical structure will respond to forces, pressure, heat, and other loads before you build it. I've applied it primarily through SolidWorks Simulation, working through stress analysis, displacement, and factor-of-safety calculations on real engineering problems.

What I can do

Set up static structural simulations, define material properties and boundary conditions, apply loads and fixtures, interpret von Mises stress and displacement results, and use FEA output to drive design changes.

Applied to

Baja SAE half-shaft redesign — used FEA to validate that the new design could withstand anticipated tension loads before fabrication. Also applied to 3DPAC airframe structural checks.

Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)

CFD simulates how fluids (air, water, gas) flow around or through objects. For an aerospace-adjacent engineer, this is directly applicable to airframe design, duct geometry, and any problem where drag, lift, or pressure distribution matters.

What I can do

Set up flow simulations, define boundary conditions and fluid properties, interpret pressure coefficient and velocity field results, and use CFD output to evaluate and compare design geometries.

Applied to

3DPAC airframe geometry decisions — used CFD intuition to evaluate wing and fuselage profiles. Also relevant to the Machworks afterburner diffuser design, where flow behavior through the diffuser geometry was a key design constraint.

What this means in practice

Having both FEA and CFD in my toolkit means I can close the loop between design and validation without waiting for a physical prototype. For a team like 3DPAC where materials and print time are limited, being able to simulate before you print is a real advantage. These skills complement my fabrication background — I understand both why a design is made the way it is and whether it will actually survive the loads it'll see.
